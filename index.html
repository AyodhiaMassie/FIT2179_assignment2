Malaysia's digital transition

Not Everyone Experiences the Internet Equally

Malaysia has made remarkable progress getting its people connected. But not everyone has benefitted equally. Access to the internet — and everything it unlocks — is still far from equal.

Chapter 1

Malaysia went online in a generation

The story begins with a national success: internet access grew quickly over two decades and became part of ordinary Malaysian life.

Chapter 2

The internet became part of everyday life

Access matters because the internet is no longer something people use occasionally. It shapes how people communicate, learn, shop, work, and spend time.

The internet economy

The pandemic effect

The sharp rise in heavier internet usage during 2020 likely reflects how lockdowns pushed work, entertainment, and social interaction online — habits that continued even after the pandemic eased.

Chapter 3

Not everyone is online

For many people living in cities, the internet is something they rarely have to think about. But for many Malaysians, a reliable connection remains out of reach, and with it, the opportunities it carries.

The difference

The regional divide

Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya recird the highest internet access rates, while Kelantan, Sabah, and Sarawak show how national progress can still leave regional gaps.

Two different realities

Urban access is close to universal, but the rural figure remains lower. That difference matters when services, education, and work keep moving online.

Connected countries tend to do better

Malaysia sits among more developed Asian countries with high internet penetration, pointing to a simple pattern: the more connected a country, the better its people tend to live.

Chapter 4

Connectivity is tied to opportunity

Unequal access wouldn't matter as much if the internet were just entertainment. But the data shows something harder to brush off — whether you look globally or state by state across Malaysia, connectivity and quality of life move in the same direction.

Opportunity, unevenly shared

The pattern holds within Malaysia too. Wealthier states like Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya have both higher incomes and stronger internet access, while Sabah and Sarawak continue to lag behind.

Chapter 5

Progress is happening, but unevenly

If connectivity shapes opportunity, the next question is whether places with less access are catching up. Recent trends show improvement, but the gains are still not enough to close every gap.

Progress, but uneven

Nearly every state improves from 2021 to 2024, but Sabah and Sarawak remain behind the country's highest-access states.

Catching up

Rural internet access is improving faster, but a noticeable gap with urban areas still remains.

Chapter 6

Malaysia in the global picture

Malaysia’s progress is real, and it shows on the world stage. The country is now among the most connected globally. But the story is not finished — earlier chapters show that national progress means little if some communities are still left behind.

The bigger picture

Malaysia now sits well above the global average and alongside some of the world’s most connected countries. But the earlier chapters show that strong national progress can still hide local gaps.
